# Minecraft adapter completion player checks

Date: 2026-10-21.

These checks exercise the [Minecraft adapter completion specification](../specs/2026-10-21-minecraft-adapter-completion.md) in the real client. The agent running them acts as the player through the Minecraft window. The agent runs Java, Gradle and automated tests separately; none of them belong in these checks.

## Before you start

1. Install the newly built PackSage JAR through the normal closed-instance copying workflow, then start Minecraft.
2. Open the existing singleplayer world. It allows cheats, so use `/give`, `/effect`, `/summon`, `/clear` and creative mode for setup.
3. Keep the developer prompt override cleared. Check 4 depends on the `active_effect` clause in the assistant instructions, which is a prompt change: test it through the in-game editor workflow (middle-click the settings gear) described in `AGENTS.md` before treating it as accepted, then verify the installed default with the override cleared.
4. Open a new PackSage chat for each check unless the check says otherwise, so earlier answers cannot supply evidence.
5. For every check record the question, the tool calls (registry, recipe, inventory and `read_evidence` calls), the returned evidence, the source pills and the reply. A passing reply is grounded in fresh evidence from those calls, not in remembered game knowledge, and includes useful clickable links where the tools supplied targets.

## Feature checks

1. Enchantment costs and mechanics. Setup: none. Ask: `What does Sharpness V add to damage, and what enchanting levels can roll it?` Look for a `search_registry` call for enchantments returning the Sharpness record with per-level `levels` (`level`, `minCost`, `maxCost`) and decoded `effectMechanics` with per-level damage additions. The reply states the addition per level and the cost range for level V as reported by the evidence, not recalled. Any entry reported `bounded` or `opaque` is mentioned as a limit.
2. Eligibility. Setup: `/give @s minecraft:diamond_pickaxe[enchantments={levels:{"minecraft:efficiency":3}}]` and hold it in the main hand. Ask: `Which enchantments can still go on the pickaxe I'm holding?` Look for a `read_evidence` call on the `inventory:` handle of the held slot returning `enchantmentEligibility` with `enchantable`, `supported` and `status`. The reply lists supported enchantments such as Unbreaking, Mending, Fortune and Silk Touch, does not misreport Fortune and Silk Touch as conflicting with Efficiency (they conflict only with each other), and shows Efficiency as already present at level 3. An already-enchanted item reports `enchantable: false` (the vanilla enchanting-table rule) while `supported` still lists the enchantments an anvil could apply; the reply must not claim nothing more can be added. Repeat with a plain diamond sword in hand to confirm the answer follows the held item.
3. Declared modifiers. Setup: none. Ask: `How much faster does Speed II make me?` Look for a `mob_effect` registry record with `attributeModifiers` for amplifiers 0 to 3. The reply says 40% movement speed for Speed II, citing the effect definition. The record reports `mechanicsStatus: callbacks_not_interpreted`, and the reply must not claim callback mechanics were read.
4. Active effects. Setup: `/effect clear @s`, then `/effect give @s minecraft:speed 40 1`. Ask: `What effects do I have and how long are they left?` Look for a `search_registry` call with registry `active_effect` returning an `active_effect:minecraft:speed` record with `durationTicks`, `amplifier` 1 and `attributeModifiers`. The reply names Speed II and a remaining time consistent with the seconds elapsed. Wait until the effect expires (about 40 seconds), then ask the same question in the same chat. Expect a fresh `active_effect` search or `read_evidence` call, not a replay of the earlier record, and a reply that no longer lists Speed (or says there are no active effects). A `read_evidence` on the expired handle returns the unknown-reference result.
5. Potion items. Setup: `/give @s minecraft:lingering_potion[potion_contents={potion:"minecraft:strong_strength"}]`, `/give @s minecraft:tipped_arrow[potion_contents={potion:"minecraft:strong_strength"}]` and `/give @s minecraft:potion[potion_contents={potion:"minecraft:strength"}]`. Ask: `What does each potion item I'm carrying do?` Look for inventory evidence with `potionContents` on all three stacks: `durationScale` 0.25 on the lingering potion, 0.125 on the tipped arrow and 1.0 on the potion. The reply states the effect and amplifier of each and explains the lingering and arrow duration scales. The scale is not applied to instant effects.
6. Component-distinct variants. Setup: `/give @s minecraft:iron_sword[custom_name='"Alpha"']` and `/give @s minecraft:iron_sword[custom_name='"Beta"',enchantments={levels:{"minecraft:sharpness":2}}]`. Ask: `Do I have two different swords?` Look for two inventory entries with different `componentFingerprint` values. The reply says they are distinct variants and names what differs (name, enchantment), not one stack of two iron swords.
7. Recipe previews. Setup: place a crafting table, give 3 oak planks and 2 sticks, and arrange a wooden pickaxe in the grid, leaving the screen open. Ask: `What's in this crafting table?` Look for an opened-menu observation whose details list `excludedProjections` containing the result slot as `recipe_preview`, with `COMPLETE` coverage. The reply lists the grid ingredients and does not count the wooden pickaxe output as stored. Repeat at a stonecutter: run `/give @s minecraft:cobblestone 4` and `/give @s minecraft:stonecutter`, place the stonecutter, put the 4 cobblestone in the input and the stairs recipe selected; its output slot (menu slot 1) must also be excluded as `recipe_preview`.
8. Brewing by name. Setup: none. In a new chat, with no hints about internals or item IDs, ask: `How do I brew a Potion of Strength?` Look for a `search_recipe` call whose result titles use localized names (for example `Potion of Strength brewing transformation`) and whose `detail` shows the scan position (`scanned=... phase=... matches=...`). If a page is empty with a cursor, the assistant continues instead of concluding. The reply gives the input potion, reagent and result. The brewing plan is checked in 8b.
8b. Brewing plan. Setup: none. In a new chat ask: `Plan how to brew 3 Potions of Strength.` Look for a `plan_recipe` call whose brewing route record reports `batches: null`, `producedQuantity: null` and the `operationSemantics` text `brewing: up to 3 bottles per operation, one reagent per operation; operation count depends on bottle grouping and is not computed`. The reply explains that up to three bottles brew per operation with one reagent and does not state a computed batch or operation count as fact.
## Carried-forward entity checks

These need the player's world and come from the specification's live acceptance section. Donkeys and mules have a saddle slot and chest cargo but no armor slot, so the replies must not report an armor section.

9. Donkey and mule, opened. Setup: `/summon minecraft:donkey ~ ~ ~ {CustomName:'"Donkey"',Tame:1b,ChestedHorse:1b,SaddleItem:{id:"minecraft:saddle",count:1},Items:[{Slot:2,id:"minecraft:diamond",count:5}]}` and `/summon minecraft:mule ~2 ~ ~ {CustomName:'"Mule"',Tame:1b,ChestedHorse:1b,SaddleItem:{id:"minecraft:saddle",count:1},Items:[{Slot:2,id:"minecraft:iron_ingot",count:7}]}`. Open the inventory screens of Donkey and Mule in turn (sneak and right-click) and ask: `What is this animal carrying?` Expect a saddle section holding the saddle and a cargo section holding the diamonds (donkey) or iron ingots (mule), mule coverage reported and horse-slot metadata labelled correctly.
10. Donkey and mule, targeted. Close the screen, look at Donkey without opening it and ask the same question; repeat for Mule. Expect targeted storage evidence naming the same saddle and cargo.
11. Donkey and mule, ridden. Mount Donkey, then Mule, with no screen open, and ask: `What is my mount carrying?` Expect the ridden entity's saddle and cargo, correct for each animal.
12. Native chest boat. Setup: `/give @s minecraft:oak_chest_boat`, place it and put a few distinct items inside. Look at it without opening it and ask: `What is in the boat I'm looking at?` Then board it and ask: `What is in the boat I'm riding?` Expect targeted and ridden contents matching the chest.
13. Switching mounts. Setup: summon a donkey with 15 distinct stacks by running `/summon minecraft:donkey ~ ~ ~ {CustomName:'"Cargo"',Tame:1b,ChestedHorse:1b,SaddleItem:{id:"minecraft:saddle",count:1}}`, then one command per cargo slot, in this order: `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.0 with minecraft:diamond`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.1 with minecraft:emerald`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.2 with minecraft:gold_ingot`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.3 with minecraft:iron_ingot`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.4 with minecraft:copper_ingot`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.5 with minecraft:coal`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.6 with minecraft:redstone`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.7 with minecraft:lapis_lazuli`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.8 with minecraft:quartz`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.9 with minecraft:amethyst_shard`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.10 with minecraft:apple`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.11 with minecraft:bread`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.12 with minecraft:stick`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.13 with minecraft:flint`, `/item replace entity @e[type=minecraft:donkey,name=Cargo,limit=1,sort=nearest] horse.14 with minecraft:clay_ball`. Every setup command must stay under 256 characters (the chat input limit); confirm all 15 stacks by opening Cargo's inventory before starting, and the tamed saddled Mule from check 9. Ride Cargo and ask: `List everything my mount is carrying, 5 items at a time.` If the assistant does not choose a limit of 5 itself, tell it to use one. Watch the storage result for a non-null `nextCursor` with fewer than 15 entries returned. Do not continue yet. Dismount, mount Mule, then tell the assistant to continue the earlier listing with that `nextCursor`. Expect the old continuation to be rejected (an invalid or stale cursor result, not more Cargo items), a fresh read that resolves Mule, and a reply describing Mule's contents, not Cargo's.

## Recording

Record the actual artifact hash, profile, questions, tool and evidence observations, source pills and remaining limits in a document under `docs/validation/` with a date-prefixed name. Record failures separately from passing cases. A passing answer does not establish behavior for third-party adapters or server-side rules the client cannot read.
