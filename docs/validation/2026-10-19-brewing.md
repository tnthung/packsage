## Delivered bounds and semantics

Discovery, continuations and evidence reads use the current level's brewing source. Each vanilla/custom rule table is capped at 8,192 rows; structural reads visit at most 64 container entries and 256 potion registry entries. Candidate projection is capped at 512 per call, or 4,096 with an output-item filter. Ingredients retain at most 64 declared alternatives. These limits produce partial coverage rather than a complete absence claim.

Locators include phase, row, variant and a fingerprint. An unchanged late rule is read directly, including the tested 5,000th custom rule. A moved-rule fallback scans at most 4,096 candidates; a rule outside that fallback can become unavailable. Removed or changed rules cannot replay the old record. Opaque rules are freshly readable without invoking their executable methods, and do not match an exact output-item filter.

Component preflight checks at most 16 serialized patch entries and a conservative 16,384-byte estimate for the supported simple component forms. Named potion contents and ordinary literal custom names are preserved. Other or oversized component forms return an explicit bounded marker with partial coverage; inherited default components are not mistaken for serialized changes.

Supported brewing records participate in the existing bounded planner through an independent candidate allowance. Potion components and declared output counts remain distinct, while unknown input quantities, batch/fuel/remainder constraints, overlapping rules and client authority keep the routes partial. Synthetic brewing locators are not presented as registered datapack recipe IDs.

## Acceptance limits and follow-up

The installed native reader passed the focused vanilla discovery, continuation, fresh-read and component-preserving conversion checks. Ordinary player-question discovery failed on the first GPT-6-Luna Extra high trial and passed on the subsequent GPT-6.1-Sol Medium comparison with the same prompt. The earlier Luna retry needed explicit native output-filter and continuation guidance and had one cursor-copy error. Current brewing record titles are generic (`minecraft:potion brewing transformation`), while payloads retain registry IDs; localized multiword names are not supplied to their literal text matcher. Discovery behavior, exact cursor copying and player-facing citation labels need follow-up rather than a claim of general model reliability.

The bounded planner kept the result partial and input/fuel totals unknown, but its generic payload still reported `batches=3`, `producedQuantity=3` and `requestedQuantity=3` for one-bottle transformations. That batch field does not establish three brewing operations, and the answer's phrase "a batch of three" does not validate its numeric meaning. Item-ID targeting also did not select Strength, and the output bound excluded its later route. Exact operational batch counts and a complete named-potion plan are not accepted by this check.

A read-only inspection of installed mod JARs did not establish a concrete NeoForge custom brewing-stand fixture. Create and Immersive Engineering consume current vanilla/custom brewing tables to derive machine recipes; those references alone do not show them registering a custom brewing rule. Declarative custom entries and opaque implementations therefore retain automated-only acceptance here. This scan does not prove the semantic absence of all custom registrations.

No physical brewing operation, JEI recipe-screen comparison, live rule mutation/removal or remote-server comparison was performed. The four Luna answers and separate Sol comparison remain available in their test conversations, with Minecraft left paused in PackSage. Existing conversations and developer prompt were preserved; model/effort changed only for the player-requested comparison. No implementation change or replacement JAR was made during live testing.

The supported scope is vanilla named potion/container conversions and exact declarative NeoForge `BrewingRecipe` entries. Unknown custom implementations/ingredients remain opaque. Client-loaded rules do not establish effective remote-server rules; overlapping custom rules retain precedence limits. Named potion targeting, exact shared-batch material/fuel totals and arbitrary custom mechanics are outside the existing planner's verified contract.
